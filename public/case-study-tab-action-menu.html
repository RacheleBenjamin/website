Testing in the developer environment to see how behavior evolves

How does Tab Central (Switcher & Transient variants) help horizontal tab users navigate tab clutter, discover different tab tools, and explore different states?

Study 2 Results · Preference

Participants were split between Switcher and Transient

Chose Switcher

"I have no need for the dropdown menu in Transient. It doesn't feel intuitive."
"I think the Transient feature takes up too much screen space."
"[Switcher] means less clicks for the same results."

Chose Transient

"[Pinned] wasn't my favorite mode because it takes up a lot of real estate on this side."
"it presents the feature in a less in-your-face fashion. I like the dropdown menu and pinning functionality."
"Transient having a pop-up that appears when you click the icon is condensed, but very useful."

Chose neither

"They seem exactly the same to be honest."
"I do not see vertical tabs being something that I explore any further."
"If anything Switcher is even worse."

Three participants didn't pick up on the difference.

What else I learned… the challenges they face today

Though slow switching was an issue (~5%) the number one pain point was High CPU load and crashing.
Study 3 Results

Kano analysis reveals quick switching is a win, but not a huge win.

Recommendation

Build Quick Switching if it won't compound other pain points (CPU load)

Outcome

The Engineer was in the room and knew beyond a doubt that the MVP for Quick Switching would create higher CPU load.

I highlighted a quick-switching risk, and laid the groundwork for future learnings.
